A **service account** is an identity the team owns, for CI, scripts and headless agents. Its keys (`omp_sa_…`) don't depend on anyone staying on the team: the team's owners and admins are responsible for it. Use one for anything that runs unattended, instead of a person's [API key](/docs/use/api-keys). Service accounts *use* gateways. To let a pipeline *manage* the team's servers (register, refresh, publish), use a [publisher token](/docs/teams/publisher-tokens).

Team owners and admins manage them under **Service accounts**, which has two tabs: **Accounts** and **Connections** (the team's [shared connections](/docs/teams/service-connections)). Members and platform staff can see them. Only owners, admins and platform staff see an account's keys. Each account's page has the tabs **Overview**, **Keys**, **Connections** and **Settings**.

## Create one

**Service accounts** › **Create service account**:

- **Name**: 1 to 64 lower-case letters, digits and dashes, such as `ci-release-bot`, unique in the team. Calls show it as who called.
- **Description**: what it's for and where its keys are deployed, so the next owner knows.
- **Role**: **Member** (reaches the capabilities open to every member of the team; enough for most automation) or **Editor** (also reaches capabilities that need editors or above). The role only counts for capabilities' minimum roles. It never makes the account an owner or admin, and it can't change anything in the team.
- **Enabled**: turned off, its keys are refused on their next request, until it's turned on again.
- **Account tool ceiling**: the tool scope every key of the account is limited to ([below](#account-tool-ceiling)). **Everything** by default.

A team can have up to 100 service accounts.

## What it can use

A service account can use:

- the **team's own gateways**, with its role for minimum roles; and
- **platform gateways granted to its team**. Grants to people or SSO groups don't cover it, and a platform gateway can't be granted to one service account.

The account's **Overview** › **Access** lists the gateways its keys reach now, with how many capabilities it reaches on each.

**Tools that need an account** on another service, a [user authorization requirement](/docs/teams/user-authorization) or a server in OAuth mode, work through a team [shared connection](/docs/teams/service-connections): an owner or admin connects a shared account, assigns it to the service account and allows it for the server. Without one, the call is refused with JSON-RPC `-32010` and a `service_connection_*` reason. A service account never uses a person's own connection. The **Access** card says how many capabilities need credentials the account doesn't have, and points to the **Connections** tab.

Servers that want [each person's own key](/docs/use/keys-for-servers) stay person-only: a service account's call is refused with `-32010` (`service_account`). A server in the [portal's own OAuth client](/docs/teams/register-a-server#the-portals-own-oauth-client) mode is different: its credential belongs to the server, so a service account can call its tools like any caller, with the same token everyone else gets.

**Calls that need approval.** When an [approval rule](/docs/teams/approval-rules) matches a service account's call, the call isn't forwarded and a request is created. The team's owners and admins follow it under Workspace › **Approvals** › **Requested** and are told when it's decided; once approved, the service account retries the identical call. By default they can't approve it themselves. See [Approving sensitive tool calls](/docs/use/approvals).

### Account tool ceiling

**Account tool ceiling** (on the create form and on **Settings**) limits which tools every key of the account may call, by [tool scope](/docs/use/tool-scopes): **Everything** (no restriction at this layer), **Read only** (only tools classified as read) or **Custom** (classes and exact tool names). It "applies immediately to every key together with its own ceiling": a key's own scope can only narrow it further, even when the key selects **Everything**. Prompts and resources are unaffected.

### Retired relay accounts

Accounts that relayed calls for an external agent platform before v0.4.0 show a **Retired** badge and the notice **Permanently retired**: "This legacy external-platform relay cannot run calls or be enabled." They are read-only, their keys were revoked, and they can't be given new keys. Create a new ordinary service account and key instead.

## Keys

On the **Keys** tab, **Create key**:

- **Name**: where it's deployed, for example "GitHub Actions release workflow". Call records show it.
- **Expires after (days)**: keys always expire, from 1 to 365 days (90 by default).
- **Limit to gateways (optional)**: the key works only on these gateways (at most 50, each one the account can use now).
- **Tool scope**: **Everything**, **Read only** or **Custom**, as for a [personal API key](/docs/use/tool-scopes). The dialog shows the account's ceiling above it, which applies together with the key's scope.

The **Keys** tab shows each key's **Tool scope**, and the account's ceiling at the top.

The secret is shown **once**. Send it like a personal key: `Authorization: Bearer omp_sa_…`. See [Use from your own agents](/docs/get-started/use-from-your-agents).

### Rotating a key

An account has at most **two active keys**, so you can rotate without downtime:

1. Create a new key. The dialog then shows "Next: finish the rotation".
2. Deploy the new key wherever the old one runs.
3. Revoke the old key. Whatever still uses it stops working on its next request.

## What servers see

MCP servers get an identity token with `principal_type: service_account`, `sub` set to the account's ID, `name` set to its name, `teams` set to its own team, and no email. A call that uses a shared connection also carries the `provider` claim, and the shared account's token in `MCP-Gateway-Provider-Token` (or, for a server in OAuth mode, in `Authorization`). See [The identity token](/docs/server-authors/identity-token).

## Records

Calls are recorded under the account's name and the key used. A call that waited for a person's approval is recorded as **Needs approval**. In the web app, a team gateway's **Activity** tab (owners and admins) can be filtered by service account, and platform staff can filter **Admin › Calls** by any team's service account (**Calls in Admin** on the account's page). The team's call list in the management API (`GET /v1/teams/{team}/calls`) includes every call of the team's service accounts through any gateway, including platform gateways granted to the team and refusals on other teams' gateways.

## When keys stop working

The next request is refused (`401`) when the account is turned off or deleted, its team is archived, or the key is revoked or expired. Deleting the account deletes its keys; its call records keep its name.
