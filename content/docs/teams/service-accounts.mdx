A **service account** is an identity the team owns, for CI, scripts and headless agents. Its keys (`omp_sa_…`) don't depend on anyone staying on the team: the team's owners and admins are responsible for it. Use one for anything that runs unattended, instead of a person's [API key](/docs/use/api-keys). Service accounts *use* gateways. To let a pipeline *manage* the team's servers (register, refresh, publish), use a [publisher token](/docs/teams/publisher-tokens).

Team owners and admins manage them under **Service accounts**; members and platform staff can see them. Only owners, admins and platform staff see an account's keys.

## Create one

**Service accounts** › **Create service account**:

- **Name**: 1 to 64 lower-case letters, digits and dashes, such as `ci-release-bot`, unique in the team. Calls show it as who called.
- **Description**: what it's for and where its keys are deployed, so the next owner knows.
- **Role**: **Member** (reaches the capabilities open to every member of the team; enough for most automation) or **Editor** (also reaches capabilities that need editors or above). The role only counts for capabilities' minimum roles. It never makes the account an owner or admin, and it can't change anything in the team.
- **Enabled**: turned off, its keys are refused on their next request, until it's turned on again.

A team can have up to 100 service accounts.

## What it can use

A service account can use:

- the **team's own gateways**, with its role for minimum roles; and
- **platform gateways granted to its team**. Grants to people or SSO groups don't cover it, and a platform gateway can't be granted to one service account.

The account's **Overview** › **Access** lists the gateways its keys reach now, with how many capabilities it reaches on each.

Service accounts **can't use tools that act as a person**: capabilities with a [user authorization requirement](/docs/teams/user-authorization), tools of servers in OAuth mode and tools of servers that want [each person's own key](/docs/use/keys-for-servers). Their calls are refused with JSON-RPC `-32010` (`service_account`). Those capabilities stay listed, marked "Service accounts can't use this". The one exception is a [relay account](#relay-accounts), when an agent platform names the person a call is for. A server in the [portal's own OAuth client](/docs/teams/register-a-server#the-portals-own-oauth-client) mode is different: its credential belongs to the server, so a service account can call its tools like any caller, with the same token everyone else gets.

## Relay accounts

A platform admin can mark a service account as the **relay** for a trusted [agent platform](/docs/administer/agent-platforms) such as Pi Durable Platform. The platform holds the account's key, and each call it forwards can carry a signed token naming the person the agent acts for. Such a call is **that person's call, limited to what the relay account could reach**: the person's own access and the account's, whichever is lower, with the person's own connections and their name in the call log.

What team owners and admins see and can do:

- A marked account shows **Relay for** and the platform's name in the list and an **Agent platform relay** card on its page. **Only a platform admin can mark or clear the relay**; you see the mark and can't change it. Marking and clearing are audited.
- **You still decide the reach.** The relay's role (**Member** or **Editor**), its enabled switch, its key's gateway limits and the gateways its team can use set the most any agent can do through it. Turn the account off or revoke its key and its next request is refused, so the platform's agents stop.
- **You hold the key.** The team's owners and admins are responsible for it, as for any service account key.
- **The people agents act for appear in your calls.** The team's call list shows each call as the agent, the platform and the person ("for Ada Lovelace"), or "no person named" when the platform names nobody. That is the point of an audit.
- **A call that names nobody** (a team agent's shared thread or background work) is the account's own call, as before, and is refused for a capability that [requires a present person](/docs/teams/capabilities#require-a-present-person).

The other tools that act as a person still need that person's connection: an agent never gets a consent link, but the person can [allow a server from My connections](/docs/use/connecting-accounts#allow-servers-from-my-connections).

## Keys

On the **Keys** tab, **Create key**:

- **Name**: where it's deployed, for example "GitHub Actions release workflow". Call records show it.
- **Expires after (days)**: keys always expire, from 1 to 365 days (90 by default).
- **Limit to gateways (optional)**: the key works only on these gateways (at most 50, each one the account can use now).

The secret is shown **once**. Send it like a personal key: `Authorization: Bearer omp_sa_…`. See [Use from your own agents](/docs/get-started/use-from-your-agents).

### Rotating a key

An account has at most **two active keys**, so you can rotate without downtime:

1. Create a new key. The dialog then shows "Next: finish the rotation".
2. Deploy the new key wherever the old one runs.
3. Revoke the old key. Whatever still uses it stops working on its next request.

## What servers see

MCP servers get an identity token with `principal_type: service_account`, `sub` set to the account's ID, `name` set to its name, `teams` set to its own team, and no email. On a relayed person call it is the person's token instead, with `client` set to this account's name and an `act` claim naming the agent. See [The identity token](/docs/server-authors/identity-token).

## Records

Calls are recorded under the account's name and the key used. In the web app, a team gateway's **Activity** tab (owners and admins) can be filtered by service account, and platform staff can filter **Admin › Calls** by any team's service account (**Calls in Admin** on the account's page). The team's call list in the management API (`GET /v1/teams/{team}/calls`) includes every call of the team's service accounts through any gateway, including platform gateways granted to the team and refusals on other teams' gateways.

## When keys stop working

The next request is refused (`401`) when the account is turned off or deleted, its team is archived, or the key is revoked or expired. Deleting the account deletes its keys; its call records keep its name.
