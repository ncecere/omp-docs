**Released 2026-10-29.** [GitHub release](https://github.com/ncecere/open-mcp-portal/releases/tag/v0.6.0) · image `ghcr.io/ncecere/open-mcp-portal:v0.6.0` · migrations `00043` to `00045` (all expand-only).

The first release of the v0.6 line, after [v0.5.1](/docs/releases/v0-5-1): a way to ask for a gateway instead of asking an admin by email, and grants that end on their own. **Nothing changes until a platform admin makes a gateway requestable;** existing grants stay permanent. Read [Upgrading](#upgrading) first.

## What changed

- **Access requests** (#32, decision 83). A platform admin can make a platform gateway **requestable** on its **Requests** tab (off by default). Every signed-in person then sees its name and description, not its tools or servers, on **Access requests** › **Request access**, and can ask for access for themselves or for a team they own or administer, with a reason. Team gateways don't take requests, and `/mcp/{slug}` still hides gateways you can't use. See [Access requests](/docs/use/access-requests) and [Access requests for platform gateways](/docs/administer/access-requests).
- **Stewards.** Besides platform admins, up to 50 named people per gateway can decide its requests. A steward decides that gateway's requests and nothing else: no admin rights, no direct grants, no gateway edits. Nobody decides their own request, platform admins included.
- **Decisions create normal grants.** Approving creates an ordinary user or team grant in the same step as the decision and its audit entry. A team grant covers the team's current and future members and its service accounts. An existing grant is never widened or extended by a request. Denying or withdrawing creates nothing.
- **Grants that expire.** Whoever approves a request, or adds a grant directly, can let it expire after 1 to 365 days; without a choice it stays permanent. An expired grant stops working at once, without waiting for a cleanup job. Expiry stops later calls; it can't stop a call already running.
- **Private by design.** Reasons and decision notes are seen only by the requester and the current reviewers. Notices (`access.requested`, `access.decided`) go to the inbox and email, never to webhooks, and carry only the request ID and status. Audit entries record the decision and expiry, never the reason. A person can have 10 requests pending, and must wait 24 hours after a denial before asking for the same access again. Every step needs a browser session; automation tokens can't take part.

## Upgrading

1. **Back up** the database together with its keys, then pin the new image (verify its signature first, see [The image](#the-image)). The init container applies `00043` to `00045`. **Do not use `goose down` for a rollback.**
2. **Finish the rollout and drain every older serving and background-worker replica before you make a gateway requestable or create an expiring grant.** v0.5.1 runs against the new schema but doesn't enforce expiry or send the new notices.
3. Decide deliberately which gateways become requestable and who their stewards are: a requestable gateway's name and description become visible to everyone signed in, and a steward can widen that gateway's access.
4. Going back to an older image doesn't keep expiry working: revoke or pause expiring grants before you do, and turn requests off. Don't turn temporary grants into permanent ones by accident.
5. From v0.4.x or earlier, follow [Upgrading in v0.5.0](/docs/releases/v0-5-0#upgrading) as well. There are no new settings.

[Upgrading from v0.5.1](/docs/operate/migrations-and-upgrades#upgrading-from-v051) has the same points with the details.

## Known limitations

Carried over from [v0.5.1](/docs/releases/v0-5-1) unless marked new:

- **Request records keep reasons and notes (new)** until the requester, the gateway or the team is deleted; suspending or archiving doesn't erase them. They don't follow the 90-day rule for approval receipts.
- **Expiry doesn't stop a call in flight (new).** It prevents later calls only.
- **"Used" means the approval was spent,** not that the upstream call succeeded; pending approvals can lapse after unrelated changes; governed calls fail closed while the approval lock is busy.
- **Not run against real clients and providers.** VS Code, Cursor, a real Google or Microsoft provider, a Slack webhook, a mail server and a pilot with a real team are still open.
- Remote servers only (no stdio); development software that has not run in production.
